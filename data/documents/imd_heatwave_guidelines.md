# India Meteorological Department (IMD) - Official Heatwave Criteria & Scientific Methodology
Ministry of Earth Sciences, Government of India

## 1. Climatological Normals & Baseline Criteria
Heatwaves are meteorological phenomena characterized by abnormally high ambient temperatures persisting for consecutive days. Because human physiological tolerance varies across geographic and climatic zones, the IMD establishes distinct regional criteria:
- **Plains:** Maximum temperature threshold >= 40.0°C.
- **Coastal Stations:** Maximum temperature threshold >= 37.0°C.
- **Hilly Regions:** Maximum temperature threshold >= 30.0°C.

## 2. Departure-From-Normal vs Absolute Temperature Thresholds
A heatwave is declared when either departure criteria OR absolute criteria are satisfied:
1. **Departure from Climatological Normal:**
   - **Heat Wave:** Departure from normal maximum temperature is between +4.5°C and +6.4°C.
   - **Severe Heat Wave:** Departure from normal maximum temperature is >= +6.5°C.
2. **Absolute Maximum Temperature (irrespective of normal):**
   - **Heat Wave:** Actual maximum temperature reaches >= 45.0°C.
   - **Severe Heat Wave:** Actual maximum temperature reaches >= 47.0°C.

## 3. Coastal Station Rules (Specific to Mumbai - Colaba & Santacruz)
For coastal meteorological observatories like Mumbai:
- Qualifying Condition: The actual maximum temperature must reach at least 37.0°C.
- Even if departure from normal exceeds +4.5°C, a heatwave is NOT declared if the actual maximum temperature remains below 37.0°C.
- Example: If normal maximum temperature for May is 33.5°C and actual temperature is 36.8°C (departure +3.3°C), heatwave criteria are not met.
- If actual temperature reaches 38.2°C (departure +4.7°C >= 4.5°C and actual >= 37.0°C), a **Coastal Heat Wave** is officially declared.

## 4. Distinction Between Satellite Land Surface Temperature (LST) and IMD Air Temperature
- **IMD Ambient Air Temperature (T2m):** Measured at 2 meters above ground within a double-louvered wooden Stevenson screen shield, representing thermodynamic kinetic energy of free air in the shade.
- **Satellite Land Surface Temperature (LST):** Measured via thermal infrared radiance by radiometers (USGS Landsat 8/9 Thermal Infrared Sensor TIRS-2 Band 10, NASA MODIS MOD11A1). It measures the radiative skin temperature of physical ground surfaces (asphalt pavement, metal rooftops, concrete structures, bare soil).
- **Urban Heat Island Divergence:** During peak insolation in Mumbai, concrete surfaces and tin roofs can reach LST of 46°C to 52°C, while ambient Stevenson screen air temperature is 36°C to 38°C.
- **Scientific Protocol:** Satellite LST provides high-resolution spatial mapping of microclimatic surface exposure and urban heat island intensity, but must NEVER be conflated with or substituted for Stevenson screen air temperature when declaring official IMD heatwaves.
