# Maharashtra State Heat Wave Action Plan 2024 (MS-HWAP 2024)
Department of Relief and Rehabilitation, Government of Maharashtra

## 1. Executive Summary & Administrative Mandate
The Maharashtra State Heat Wave Action Plan 2024 provides an operational framework for disaster management authorities, municipal corporations (including the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation - BMC), and district administrations to minimize morbidity and mortality associated with extreme heat events. The plan coordinates early warnings, public health responses, inter-agency communication, and infrastructure adaptations across urban and rural jurisdictions.

## 2. Early Warning Systems & Heat Alert Classifications
Early warnings are issued in coordination with the India Meteorological Department (IMD). The alert framework comprises four graded response stages:
- **Green Alert (Normal):** Maximum temperatures are within climatological normal limits. Routine seasonal monitoring is conducted.
- **Yellow Alert (Be Updated):** Mild heatwave conditions persist for 1 to 2 days. Tolerable for general public, but moderate risk for vulnerable populations such as infants, elderly individuals, outdoor workers, and persons with chronic cardiovascular or respiratory illnesses.
- **Orange Alert (Be Prepared):** Severe heatwave conditions persist for 1 to 2 days or moderate heatwave conditions persist for 3 to 4 days. High temperature combined with elevated relative humidity poses significant health risks. Municipalities must activate cooling shelters and adjust public work shifts.
- **Red Alert (Take Action):** Severe heatwave conditions persist for more than 2 days or heatwave conditions persist for more than 4 days. Extreme heat emergencies with very high risk of heat stroke, heat exhaustion, and systemic organ failure. Emergency operation centres (EOCs) operate on continuous standby.

## 3. Coastal Station Heatwave Criteria (Mumbai Context)
For coastal meteorological observatories including Mumbai (Santacruz and Colaba observatories):
- Qualifying Threshold: Actual maximum ambient air temperature must reach at least 37.0°C.
- Heat Wave Declaration: Declared when maximum temperature departure from normal is between +4.5°C and +6.4°C (provided actual temperature >= 37.0°C).
- Severe Heat Wave Declaration: Declared when maximum temperature departure from normal is >= +6.5°C (provided actual temperature >= 37.0°C).
- Absolute Maximum Threshold: When actual maximum temperature reaches >= 45.0°C regardless of departure from normal.

## 4. Municipal Directives for Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC)
1. **Cool Roof Programs:** Priority deployment of high solar-reflectance coatings (albedo >= 0.70) in high-density informal settlements and slums across Wards M/East (Govandi, Mankhurd), G/North (Dharavi), and L (Kurla) to reduce indoor surface temperatures by up to 3°C to 5°C.
2. **Dedicated Cooling Shelters:** Establish officially designated public cooling centres in municipal community centres, libraries, and public transit nodes during Orange and Red alert days. Note: These dedicated shelters must be distinguished from hospitals and parks.
3. **Public Hydration Network:** Expand BMC drinking water points (Pyaus) across high-footfall railway terminals (Dadar, Kurla, Andheri, CSMT) and outdoor labor corridors.
4. **Labor Rescheduling:** Prohibition of outdoor construction and heavy manual labor during peak radiant hours (12:00 PM to 4:00 PM) upon issuance of Orange or Red alerts. Provision of shaded rest areas with ORS (Oral Rehydration Salts) packets.
5. **Hospital Preparedness:** BMC tertiary hospitals (KEM, Sion LTMG, Nair, Cooper) and suburban hospitals (Bhabha, Shatabdi, Rajawadi) must maintain dedicated heat stroke treatment beds, cold water immersion ice bags, and intravenous fluid reserves.
