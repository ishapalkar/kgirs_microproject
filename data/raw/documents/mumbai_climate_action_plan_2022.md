# Mumbai Climate Action Plan 2022 (MCAP 2022) - Urban Heat Chapter
Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM / BMC)

## 1. Urban Heat Island (UHI) Assessment for Mumbai
Satellite thermal infrared imagery (USGS Landsat 8/9 Band 10 Surface Temperature) reveals pronounced daytime Land Surface Temperature (LST) anomalies across Mumbai's urban core and eastern suburban wards. Surface temperatures in highly built-up, non-vegetated wards exceed surrounding coastal and mangrove microclimates by 5°C to 9°C.

### Identified High Thermal Exposure Wards:
- **Ward M/East (Govandi, Deonar, Mankhurd):** Exhibits highest mean LST (41.2°C to 44.5°C in pre-monsoon May) driven by heavy industrial density, low vegetation cover (NDVI < 0.12), and dense tin-roof informal settlements surrounding Deonar.
- **Ward L (Kurla, Sakinaka):** Characterized by dense industrial estates, low tree canopy, and severe UHI intensity (mean LST 40.5°C). High vulnerability index exacerbated by Mithi River corridor flood-plain density.
- **Ward G/North (Dharavi, Mahim East):** High building footprint, limited vegetative cooling buffer (NDVI 0.14), and high population density exceeding 60,000 persons/km².
- **Ward K/East (Andheri East, MIDC, Marol):** Industrial and commercial corridors showing substantial concrete thermal mass and vehicle emission heat concentration.
- **Ward P/North (Malad West, Marve Road, Rathodi):** Dense suburban sprawl with high radiant heat absorption in western slum settlements.

### Identified Low Thermal Exposure Wards:
- **Ward R/Central (Borivali) & Ward S (Bhandup / Powai):** Bordering the Sanjay Gandhi National Park (SGNP), these wards benefit from vegetative evapotranspiration buffers, with mean LST ranging from 32.5°C to 34.8°C and high NDVI (>0.40).
- **Ward D (Malabar Hill, Grant Road) & Ward A (Colaba, Fort):** Maritime sea-breeze circulation and mature heritage tree canopy (Priyadarshini Park, Hanging Gardens) moderate both surface and ambient temperatures.

## 2. Demographic Sensitivity and Social Vulnerability
Vulnerability is strongly correlated with socio-economic marginalization:
- **Children under 5 and Adults over 60:** Represent 16% to 22% of populations in wards M/East, F/North, and L. Their thermoregulatory systems are less efficient under prolonged humid heat.
- **Informal Labor Workforce:** High concentration of construction laborers, street vendors, and delivery couriers in wards H/East, K/East, and G/North who face direct solar insolation without occupational cooling.

## 3. MCAP Adaptation Targets & Strategic Measures
1. **Urban Greening & Tree Canopy:** Increase per-capita open space by expanding pocket parks, mangrove restoration in Thane Creek, and bioswales.
2. **Cool Roof Initiative:** Target 100,000 m² of residential cool roofs in Ward M/East and Ward G/North by 2025.
3. **Public Hydration & Heat Shelters:** Equip ward municipal offices and bus terminals with free chilled drinking water points and emergency misting stations during heatwave occurrences.
